A question that keeps coming up

Many people confuse internal audit with risk assessment and assume one replaces the other. In fact they differ in purpose, timing and who carries them out, but they work together.

Risk assessment: what could happen?

  • It identifies the risks that could hinder the company's objectives: financial, operational, regulatory, reputational and others.
  • It measures the likelihood and impact of each risk and ranks them by priority.
  • It proposes controls and treatments and tracks them in a risk register that is updated regularly.
  • Management carries it out with support from the risk function; it is part of running the company.

Internal audit: do the controls actually work?

  • It independently checks whether controls and policies exist and are applied as they should be.
  • It works to an annual audit plan, usually built on the results of the risk assessment.
  • It issues findings and recommendations to the audit committee or the board, and follows them until closure.
  • Its independence is essential, so it does not manage the work it reviews. More detail in the internal audit module.

How does one complement the other?

The risk assessment shows where the biggest risk is, so internal audit focuses its effort there. Audit in turn uncovers weak controls or risks that were never spotted, which go back into the risk register. That closes the loop. To see how roles are split across the three parties, read The Three Lines of Defense.

When does your company need each?

  • Risk assessment: almost from the start, and more as you grow, raise financing or add activities.
  • Internal audit: when departments and controls multiply, when there is a board and committees, or when an investor or regulator expects independent assurance.
  • For small companies: an outside party can carry out the audit while keeping independence, and a simple risk assessment can be run each year.

Conclusion

Risk assessment looks ahead; internal audit checks reality. A mature company needs both. Start with a simple risk register, then add audit as the business and its controls grow.
