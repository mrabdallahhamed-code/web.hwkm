Board Performance Evaluation: What to Measure and How to Run It Effectively

Why evaluate the board?

A board constantly reviews the performance of executive management, but rarely reviews its own. Evaluating the performance of the board, its committees and its members shows what works and what does not, turns impressions into data, and fixes weaknesses before they become late decisions or formal meetings.

What is evaluated?

  • The board as a whole: clarity of goals and strategy, quality of discussion, and oversight of management and risk.
  • Committees: regular meetings, quality of reports and follow-up of recommendations.
  • Members: attendance, participation, independence and knowledge of the sector.
  • The chair and the secretary: running the meeting, quality of minutes and distribution of materials.

Practical indicators you can measure

  • Attendance rate, and meetings held against those planned.
  • How long before the meeting the agenda and materials reach members.
  • Share of decisions implemented on time.
  • Time taken to approve minutes after the meeting.
  • Number of decisions repeated or postponed without a reason.

How to run the process

  1. Define the areas and criteria in advance and have the board approve them.
  2. Collect members' views through a confidential survey and combine them with measurable indicators.
  3. Prepare a report summarising results, strengths and areas for improvement.
  4. Discuss it at a board meeting and approve an improvement plan with an owner and a date for each item.
  5. Repeat periodically and compare results with the previous year.

Who carries out the evaluation?

The board may do it itself, led by its chair and the nominations committee where one exists, or with the help of an independent party. An external party helps when the board wants more objectivity or when internal discussion stalls. Check what laws and instructions require for your company type, such as listed companies.

Common mistakes

  • A formal evaluation where members fill in a form and nothing is discussed.
  • No measurable indicators, so it rests on impressions.
  • Results with no improvement plan or follow-up.
  • Avoiding evaluation of the chair or senior members.

How does a digital platform help?

In HWKM meetings, attendance, decisions and their implementation are recorded, so real indicators are available for the evaluation. A governance effectiveness and performance evaluation module is part of the platform, with proactive alerts for follow-up.

Conclusion

Board evaluation is not an annual form; it is a cycle: indicators, then members' views, then an improvement plan, then follow-up. Start with simple indicators you already have, then widen them.

Note: legal and regulatory requirements vary by company type and listing status, so check the laws in force when applying.
