The Three Lines of Defense: Governance, Risk Management and Internal Audit

Abdullah Hamed — CEO & Founder · October 11, 2026

The three lines of defense model divides responsibilities between operating management, risk and compliance, and internal audit. Many companies have these functions, but not everyone knows who is responsible for what, so some work is duplicated while other gaps have no owner.

The basic idea

Risks are not managed by a single party. Each party has a different role, and the roles work together under the oversight of the board of directors.

First line: operating management

  • They carry out the daily work: department heads and employees.
  • Their responsibilities: identifying risks in their work, applying controls, and complying with policies.
  • Example: a procurement manager who applies contract approval controls is a first line.

Second line: risk management and compliance

  • Functions that set frameworks and monitor compliance, such as risk management and compliance.
  • Their responsibilities: setting the risk methodology, assessing and following up risks, monitoring compliance with regulations, and reporting to senior management.
  • They do not do the work, but they support the first line and verify that it manages its risks.

Third line: internal audit

  • An independent function that gives the board objective assurance on the effectiveness of governance, risk management and internal control.
  • Internal audit independence is essential, so it reports to the audit committee or the board and not only to executive management.
  • It examines the first and second lines and issues findings and recommendations that are followed up until closed.

The role of the board and senior management

The board oversees and sets direction, and asks for reports from the three lines; the audit committee is the link between the board and internal audit. Senior management is responsible for achieving objectives and managing risk. External parties, such as the external auditor and regulators, provide additional assurance from outside the company. The model was later developed by the Institute of Internal Auditors into the "Three Lines Model", with more emphasis on collaboration and on the aim of protecting the company and creating value.

Common mistakes

  • Merging the risk function with audit, which loses independence.
  • Treating risk as "the risk department's business alone", when the first line is primarily responsible.
  • Reports that are not followed up or closed.
  • No single register linking risks to controls and audit findings.

Small companies that cannot set up three departments need a clear division of roles, or an external party for audit, while preserving independence as far as possible.

How does a digital platform help?

Conclusion

The three lines of defense model does not require a large company. It is enough for each party to know its role and for the board to receive a clear, independent report. Start by identifying who is responsible for each line in your company, then link risks to controls and audit findings.

Note: legal and regulatory requirements vary by company type and whether it is listed, so applicable regulations should be consulted when implementing.
