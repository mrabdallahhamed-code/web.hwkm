Before we start

Penalties and legal effects differ with the company type, its rules and whether it is listed, and this article does not replace checking the laws in force. But these mistakes repeat in many companies, and can lead to liability, penalties or challenges to decisions, and they are mostly easy to prevent.

1. Missing or late minutes

A decision without minutes is hard to prove. Keep minutes for every meeting with its date, attendance, items and votes, and sign them by a set deadline.

2. A mistake in quorum or majority

Percentages differ by decision type and company type. Let the system calculate them instead of working them out by hand, and check your articles of association.

3. An incomplete or late invitation

Invitations have deadlines and attachments set by law and the company's articles. Missing them opens the meeting to challenge. Use a fixed template and a calendar.

4. Not disclosing a conflict of interest

A member votes on a deal in which they have an interest without disclosing it. Ask for a periodic declaration of interests and record the disclosure in the minutes.

5. Unwritten authorities

Who signs? Who approves large amounts? Without an authority matrix the answer becomes personal judgement, and someone carries responsibility without having the authority.

6. An outdated articles of association

A change in the company's structure or board without updating its articles. Review them at every substantial change.

7. Circulation resolutions without attachments or records

A vote is requested without the member seeing the papers. Attach the documents and record each member's vote and its date.

8. Neglecting required disclosures

In listed companies, a late or missed disclosure is among the most serious mistakes. Link material events to a disclosure checklist and a named owner.

9. No follow-up of decisions

A decision is issued but not implemented, and nobody follows it. Create a register with an owner, a date and a status for each decision.

10. Data scattered across files and messages

When documents are scattered, proof is impossible when you need it. Gather them in an organised register with access permissions.

Conclusion

Most of these mistakes are procedural, not strategic, and are fixed by organised documentation and timely alerts. Start with a quick review of the three most serious ones in your company.

Note: legal and regulatory requirements vary by company type and listing status, so check the laws in force when applying.
